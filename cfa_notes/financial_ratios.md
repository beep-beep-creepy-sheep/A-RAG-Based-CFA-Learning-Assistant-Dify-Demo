# Financial Ratios

## Definition

Financial ratios summarize relationships in financial statements. They help analysts evaluate profitability, liquidity, solvency, efficiency, and valuation.

## Formula

Common ratios:

```text
Current Ratio = Current Assets / Current Liabilities
Gross Margin = Gross Profit / Revenue
Net Profit Margin = Net Income / Revenue
Debt-to-Equity = Total Debt / Total Equity
Return on Equity = Net Income / Average Equity
```

## Intuition

Ratios make financial statement information easier to compare across time, companies, and industries. They are most useful when interpreted in context.

## Common Trap

Do not analyze ratios mechanically. Accounting policies, business models, seasonality, one-time items, leverage, and industry differences can make ratios misleading.

For liquidity, a high current ratio is not always strong if current assets are slow-moving or low quality.

## Mini Example

If a company has current assets of `500` and current liabilities of `250`, its current ratio is:

```text
500 / 250 = 2.0
```

This suggests current assets are twice current liabilities, but the analyst should still examine the quality and timing of those assets.
