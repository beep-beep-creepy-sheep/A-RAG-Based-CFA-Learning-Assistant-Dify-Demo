# Ethics: Suitability

## Definition

Suitability means an investment recommendation or action should fit the client's objectives, constraints, financial situation, risk tolerance, time horizon, liquidity needs, tax situation, and other relevant circumstances.

## Formula

There is no numerical formula. The logic is:

```text
Suitable recommendation = Client profile + Investment characteristics + Portfolio context
```

## Intuition

A product can be attractive in general but unsuitable for a specific client. The advisor must understand both the client and the product before making a recommendation.

Suitability is not just about expected return. It is about fit.

## Common Trap

Do not equate the highest expected return with the best recommendation. A high-return product may be unsuitable if it violates the client's risk tolerance, liquidity needs, time horizon, or constraints.

Another trap is evaluating an investment in isolation instead of considering the client's full portfolio.

## Mini Example

A retired client needs stable income and near-term liquidity. A highly volatile private investment with a long lockup may be unsuitable even if it has a high expected return.
