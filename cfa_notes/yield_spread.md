# Yield Spread

## Definition

Yield spread is the difference between the yield on a bond and the yield on a benchmark or comparable bond. It is often used to assess compensation for credit risk, liquidity risk, maturity differences, and embedded options.

## Formula

Basic spread:

```text
Spread = Bond Yield - Benchmark Yield
```

If a corporate bond yields `6.2%` and a comparable government bond yields `4.0%`, the spread is:

```text
6.2% - 4.0% = 2.2% = 220 basis points
```

## Intuition

Investors usually demand extra yield for taking extra risk or accepting less liquidity. A wider spread often signals higher perceived risk, weaker liquidity, or less favorable bond features.

## Common Trap

Do not interpret a higher yield as automatically better. A high spread may be compensation for higher default risk, poor liquidity, downgrade risk, or unfavorable embedded options.

For bonds with embedded options, option-adjusted spread is often more useful because it adjusts for the value of the option.

## Mini Example

Two bonds both mature in five years. A government bond yields `3%`, while a corporate bond yields `5%`.

```text
Spread = 5% - 3% = 2% = 200 basis points
```

The extra `200 bps` is compensation for risks such as credit and liquidity risk.
